Comparative Information: ASUIR Service vs. a ASUIR - Generator of Ultra-Efficient Production

As with ASUIR, a hospital, laboratory, or hotel can be viewed as a production facility that generates services rather than goods, such as a procedure, an analysis, or an X-ray. In this sense, the principles of ASUIR and ASUIR Service are similar. Staff salaries are at U.S. levels or higher, while the cost of the service (e.g., a hospital procedure) is comparable to that in Bangladesh, one of the cheapest countries in the world.

At the same time, the ASUIR service's productivity increase is not achieved by intensifying the work of service personnel, but rather through production automation—the use of robotic systems.

Step-by-Step Automation: From Manual Service Production to Production Largely Carried Out by Robotic Devices

1. People:Everything is done by people.
2. People + Ready-Made Devices: Purchasing certain devices already in use in hospitals, such as electronic queuing systems, self-service kiosks where patients can measure their own blood pressure and temperature, and IV pumps controlled by a central console, results in a reduction in support staff.
3. The Future: Robotic Devices Robotic devices - operate in two shifts, seven days a week. This will be possible once robots are approved for use in patient care.

A given option is evaluated for effectiveness in the same way as an automated control system for a production generator. The hospital considers equipment, the building, and the salaries of all employees, including the doctor, to be direct costs. The cost of medication is included in the cost of the service, and the amount is the same for all options..

For the engineer: how it is calculated

Procedures per year = chairs × hours per day ÷ (procedure + chair turnover) × days per year.

Employees = people per shift × (hours per day ÷ 8) × (days per year ÷ 250).

Doctors work daytime (usually until 5 p.m.), so their number does not change when the unit stays open longer.

Direct costs per year = wages of staff and doctors per year + purchase price of devices and robots ÷ service life + their repair and maintenance + rent of the premises.

Required productivity = direct costs per year ÷ days open per year ÷ payment for administering one procedure.

Full cost of one procedure = drug + direct costs per year ÷ procedures per year.

Break-even robot purchase price — the price at which the hospital cost per procedure in option 3 equals option 2.
