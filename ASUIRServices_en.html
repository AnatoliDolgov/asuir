ABOUTautomated management system ASUIRdriver of ultra-efficient production ASUIR — SERVICESdriver of ultra-efficient services Helpreference and formulas
The service becomes cheaper through the use of automated machines, not through making the staff work harder.

The principles of ASUIR and ASUIR Service are similar: staff salaries are at US levels or higher, while the cost of the service (in a hospital, this is a procedure) is comparable to that in Bangladesh — one of the cheapest countries in the world, where staff salaries are similar to those in the US (here, $10,000).

At the same time, increased productivity at the ASUIR service is not achieved by increasing the workload of service personnel, but by automating production processes through the use of robotic systems.

Automation, step by step: Transitioning from Manual Service Production to Production Largely Carried Out by Robotic Devices.

1. People: Everything is done by people.
2. People + ready-made devices Purchasing certain devices that are already in use in hospitals will reduce the need for support staff. These include electronic queuing systems and self-service kiosks where patients can take their own blood pressure and temperature readings, as well as IV pumps that are controlled by a central console.
3. Future: robotic systems Robotic devices will operate in two shifts, seven days a week. This will be possible once approval has been granted for the use of robots in patient care.

The effectiveness of a given option is assessed in the same way as in ASUIR — the driver of ultra-efficient production. The hospital's equipment, the building itself, and the salaries of its employees, including doctors, are considered direct costs and are factored into the cost of the service. The cost of medication is included in the service cost and remains consistent across all options.

For the engineer: how it is calculated

Hospital direct costs per day = rent + doctors + nurses + robotic complexes + chair equipment. The drug is not a direct cost: the patient pays for it; it is the "component" of the procedure.

Rent and equipment — per year ÷ working days per year; pay — per month ÷ working days per month.

Doctors and nurses — in proportion to procedures per day. Robotic complexes = procedures per day × minutes per patient ÷ (admitting hours × 60), rounded up.

Direct costs per procedure = direct costs per day ÷ procedures per day. Full cost of a procedure = drug + direct costs per procedure.

Hospital profit per month = (payment for a procedure without the drug − direct costs per procedure) × procedures per day × working days per month.

Minimum procedures per day: the allowed share × the drug cost is how much direct cost one procedure may carry; the fixed costs per day (rent, equipment, complexes) are divided by that amount minus the doctors' and nurses' cost per procedure.

Sources: rent — CBRE, Greater Los Angeles medical outpatient buildings, Q1 2026 ($5.44 per sq ft per month in West LA); space per chair — HGA (153–260 sq ft).
