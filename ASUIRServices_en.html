The same hospital sees more patients — at no extra cost.
They use the same scanner and employ the same doctors and nurses. They simply organize the work like a factory line to increase throughput.

What is the service generator?

Hospitals, labs, hotels, and laundries are also places of production. Instead of producing an item, they provide a service: a scan, a test, or a procedure. So, the same ASUIR principle applies. U.S.-level wages and higher at a Bangladesh-level price, with no tariffs or loss of profit.

The key is simple: The more services one machine and one person deliver per day, the smaller the share of the price that goes toward rent, equipment, and wages. The price approaches the cost of supplies.

Three levers for services

1. The equipment never sits idle.The machine doesn't just work one shift; it works evenings and weekends, too. In a typical hospital, a CT scanner is in use about a third of the time.
2. People work without gaps.The steps of the service are synchronized so that no one waits. Speed up the slowest step, which is usually the doctor.
3. The client participates in the service.A prepared patient who brings their own charts saves the doctor time. A 10-minute visit instead of a 15-minute visit means the doctor can see 48 people a day instead of 32.

Your service versus the "imported" one

A service doesn't arrive in a container — people travel for it. So, we compare your service here with the same service abroad such as at a clinic in Mexico, where people go for a lower price.

The bottleneck is the doctor.

As in a factory, the slowest step determines the pace of the entire process. If the first step is fast, the patient simply joins the doctor's queue sooner. The doctor must be sped up, and the steps must be synchronized. The treatment chair must be ready for the moment the visit ends.

For the engineer: how it is calculated

Cost of one scan = (scanner price ÷ service life + service + premises + technologists × wage × 12) ÷ (scans per day × working days) + supplies per scan + radiologist per scan.

Patients per doctor per day = doctor's working time ÷ visit length.
